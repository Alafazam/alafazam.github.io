Working notes:

- The claim: an "urgent-critical" ask is almost never about the ask — it's a symptom of a broken prioritization contract between teams.
- What happened at Increff when ROA forced every urgent ask to state its attention cost: 3–4 per sprint → 0.
- The second-order effect is the interesting part: stakeholders started pre-filtering themselves. Sales stopped escalating; they started scoring.
- Failure modes to cover: real emergencies still exist (production incidents ≠ roadmap urgency); how to keep the escape hatch without reopening the floodgate.
- Possible structure: symptom → diagnosis → the contract that fixes it → what changed culturally.
