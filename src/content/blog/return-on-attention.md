Running product for six products with three stakeholder groups — Sales, Success, and Engineering — taught me something uncomfortable: every prioritization framework I tried eventually collapsed into a negotiation. RICE scores got gamed. Effort estimates got argued. And every week still produced "urgent-critical" asks that reset the roadmap and burned the team's focus.

The thing nobody was pricing in was **attention**. Not engineering hours — attention. Context switches, re-planning meetings, the mental tax of a roadmap that might change any Friday. So I started scoring every incoming ask by a single question: *what return does this earn on the team's scarcest resource?*

## How it works

Every ask — from a client escalation to a shiny feature idea — gets scored by its Return on Attention, then merged and deferred against one master list. The trade-offs are explicit and shared: when something jumps the queue, everyone can see exactly what it displaced and why.

Three things changed:

- **Urgent-critical asks dropped from three or four per sprint to zero.** Not because emergencies stopped, but because most "emergencies" couldn't justify their attention cost when forced to state it.
- Prioritization became a **lens everyone uses**, not a negotiation between whoever shouts loudest.
- Teams aligned on a single, predictable set of focus areas — and stayed aligned.
